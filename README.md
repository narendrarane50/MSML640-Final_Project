## **1. Problem Statement**
Facial Expression Recognition (FER) plays a vital role in areas like human–computer interaction (HCI), healthcare, and social robotics, enabling systems to understand and respond to human emotions. However, real-world or “in-the-wild” FER systems still face persistent challenges that limit their accuracy and robustness:

1. Large Pose Variations: Significant performance degradation between frontal and profile faces.
2. Occlusions: Facial regions obscured by masks, sunglasses, or hands.
3. Limited Labeled Data: Lack of large-scale, accurately annotated emotion datasets.
4. Subtle Expressions & Class Imbalance: Difficulty capturing nuanced emotional cues and addressing skewed data distributions.

These limitations hinder the reliability of FER systems under diverse, real-world conditions. Therefore, there is a need for a robust and interpretable FER system that can perform well under pose variations, occlusions, and limited supervision.
